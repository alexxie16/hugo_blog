
I joined **SEC-06 and SEC-07** back-to-back from March to April. It is a transformative experience, I want to write things down while my memory is warm and fresh. It shifted my career direction—I left my fiat job to focus on Bitcoin, and now I’m broadening that goal toward building freedom tech software instead.


## We're all builders 

### Demo Day on Friday 

> Photo of the demo requirements

I was put off by the demo requirements at first. My friend [Victor](https://npub.world/npub18vay956v7zs5qtgc65mvn54v96cuvqv6j9fmu4cgfjqkt5vjuvjsc47nzf) encouraged me—he didn’t know Nostr that well when he first joined, but he said that he always get some ideas from talking to the people there before Thursday and he was always able to deliver. 

Also with the help of agentic AI, building a demos has become so easy.   

Usually starting Monday, the very common conversation starters are something like: "Are you ready for your demo?" and "What are you gonna demo this week", and If you didn’t have anything yet—which happened to me often—the veterans would usually throw you a bone or two.

Now that the cohort is behind me, I realize that I really liked the setup. If there were no pressure from public demo, I would not have pushed myself that hard, I would not have the demos finished / polished. Which some of them I do think are useful, and I do plan to keep working on them. I stayed up late on a few Thursdays, it is worth it. 


### Build in the spirit of Bitcoin

Bitcoin is a given. Almost everyone in the cohort is a Bitcoiner. We were there to build permissionless freedom tech. Software that empowers individuals, censorship resistant, and protects privacy.

Most people were building on Nostr, which I didn't realize the potentials and opportunities on Nostr beforehand, it's like eye-opening to me. Making money from the products are usually less of priority, but we do often bitter about it. 


### No time to sleep (sometimes to eat) 

I once heard about the academic triangle: good grades, social life, and sleep. You only get to pick two. At SEC, it was building, social life, and sleep. Same rule.

Most people were sleep-deprived most days, if not every day. That part was rough. But there was so much going on. So much energy. So much FOMO. And it was a short stint, so we kind of accepted it. Like Laurie said, paraphrasing: back in Finland, I am just a crazy person by myself. Here, I found my group of like-minded people and build something interesting. 


## Other Activities 

Monday: captain handover, setting the theme, then a hike and barbecue in the mountains. The weather could be rough, but it rarely stopped us. We often talk to different people along the way and spark lots of ideas. One guy even got hit in the head and bled on the trail, so yes, some caution is still advised.

Tuesday talks: slides, ideas, values, and a bit of self-promotion. I enjoyed learning about freedom tech related topics, also privacy, soveignty etc etc. We all had pretty strong convictions. 

Wednesday: workshops. We get our hands dirty. A lot of people shamelessly promoted their own products, which was totally fine as long as they were freedom-tech related.

PS: Most people used Linux and GrapheneOS phones. As a Mac and iPhone user, I was definitely in the minority.

## What about daily life

### Accommodation

Most people rented an Airbnb or a flat within walking distance of the station. I initially rented a room in a shared house 3 km away and planned to bike to the station. I found out that is a bad idea on day one. The hills in Madeira are no joke. I later moved to a place lower down and closer to the group.

PS: There are some spontaneous activities in cohort, if you stay far away, you might miss them, of course you can always use Bolt, whihc is surprsingly affordable in Madeira. I really enjoyed hanging out with people in SEC, we talked late into the night many times, until shop owners force us out.


### Good views and good people 

Madeira is beautiful. A lot of stunning views, a lot of hilly roads outside the beach.  

Quite a few shops accepted Bitcoin, still not as many as I would like, but enough to get around. I also finally started using Ecash there, it is quite convenient, I did not that know I could melt a Cashu token and pay over Lightning whenever I wanted.

There was also a Bitcoin walk every Saturday, we usually get 5-10 people, lots of legit Bitcoiners. 

PS: European charging sockets are used here, so you might need a travel adapter if coming from Amerca or Asia.


### Some other notes 

I heard that Madeira is one of the hardest islands to reach, and that is partly by design by hte SEC organizers. Once you get there, you do not leave that easily, so you stick with the group, haha. It filters out some half-hearted attendees.

The program and structure at SEC are also constantly evolving. A bit of chaos is expected. With the right people, that actually makes it more fun I think. 😛
