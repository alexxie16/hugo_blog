## World class hoop-jumper 

Paul has been following the default path since he was a kid, getting into great school, good grades and, high paying job with prestigious consulting company, it's a default enviable path. That's why it's very difficult for me to even think about an alternative path. 

* Got into software development, make good money, but I don't like developing in iOS.  
* Gave it up coming to the US, start over, but I was reluctant to jump ship, it's one of my biggest regret, though who knows, I might not able to find job leaving graduate school, but I didn't give it a try.   
* Waiting for green card, leaving US. 



## Work, work, work

* In 2000 years ago Greek, people consider work is a "necessary evil", so much so like we consider of the government.   

I have seen so many people they don't like their work, or their colleagues, but their life so centered around work & colleagues, it's like a abusive relationship to me. 

I'm glad I have some savings, also not hijacked by the consumerism culture, but I need to keep working to sustain the visa, it's killing me. 



## Awakening 

> “It definitely wasn't a sudden realization. It’s a little bit like having a pebble in your shoe, where you’re walking and something is off, and it’s mildly uncomfortable.”

I've never doubt that I'm a hardworking person, I do love to work, I see it as a training for my professional skills, also a way to connect with people and the society, I think most people feel this way. 

But whenever people ask me whether I like my work, I can't give a definitive answer, I got paid well and treated decently, but there is something missing, no excitment and adventure.   


## Breaking Free 

> “How are you complicit in creating the conditions you say you don’t want?” After the reflection, I knew that I was complicit. I also knew that if I wanted things to change, it was solely up to me.

This part hits me hard, deep down I know that I need to take responsibility for myself, but it's hard, it takes a lot of time and effort, I have to give up on leisure activities or even current work responsibility in some extent, I didn't, and it's already quite late, I have to take a drastic change in order to catch up. 


## Will they still love you 


We all value how other people see us, in fact, my dad mentioned this right away when I first brought up this, how would your relatives and friends think about you, if you take this "pathless path". 


> People who value comfort and security often cannot understand why anyone would willingly pursue a path that increases discomfort and uncertainty.

I don't need those people understanding me, I might success or fail, happy or regret, I can take on them on my own. 

> When everyone you know builds their lives around a steady paycheck, it is easy to lose track of what we give up for that paycheck and forget that for most of history this was not a normal state of affairs.

## The real work of your life 

### We want to be useful 

> The need to feel useful is a powerful one. This is the hidden upside of the pathless path and a reason why finding work that aligns with what matters to you and makes you feel useful is so important.
